# Evidence-first re-review: protocol

_Status: **v0.9, pilot draft, 20 Oct 2026.** Written before any row was re-coded. Frozen as v1.0 after the pilot (`docs/REVIEW-PILOT.md`); changes after the freeze are listed in § 9 with the reason._

This protocol applies METHODOLOGY § 1 under the stricter reading approved by John Milad on 20 Oct 2026 (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Evidence-first re-review…"). Where it is stricter than METHODOLOGY, it wins for the re-review; METHODOLOGY is updated to match once the re-review is signed off.

**The acid test.** If the developer itself does not claim, in its own words, that AI or machine learning did work for this molecule, the registry must not claim it either.

## 1. Unit of review

- One row of `data/molecules.csv` (strict and borderline) or `data/excluded.csv`.
- A row covering several molecules (for example ADG106/ADG116/ADG126, or an excluded group) is tested molecule by molecule. A molecule from an excluded group that passes is proposed as its own row.
- As-of date: **20 Oct 2026**. Evidence published after it does not count.

## 2. Evidence fields

Recorded for every strict and borderline row in `molecules.csv`. The best single claim is recorded: the one that comes closest to passing rules 1 and 2.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `ai_claim_quote` | Verbatim, 40 words or fewer, copied from the page at `ai_claim_url`. Ellipses only to drop words, never to join sentences from different paragraphs. |
| `ai_claim_url` | The page the quote was copied from. It must have been fetched during the review. |
| `ai_claim_by` | `developer` (the company that discovered the molecule, or its successor or acquirer), `partner` (a co-developer, licensee or the AI-platform partner named in the deal), `developer_paper` (a paper or patent authored by the developer or partner), or `third_party` (press, analysts, trackers, reviews, conferences not by the developer). A verbatim quote of a named developer executive inside a news article counts as `developer`; a paraphrase does not. |
| `ai_claim_date` | Publication date of the quoted source, ISO format. |
| `ai_step` | `target`, `design` or `both`: the step the quote says the AI did. Blank when the quote names no step. |
| `borderline_reason` | Borderline rows only, exactly one code from § 4. |

`excluded.csv` gains `exclusion_code` (§ 5). Every check is also logged in `data/audit-log.csv` (append-only; fields `ai_claim_*`, `inclusion`, `borderline_reason`, `exclusion_code`), with the source URL and a verbatim quote of 25 words or fewer.

## 3. Decision rules

A row is **strict** only if rules 1 to 3 pass and the row survives rule 4.

**Rule 1: attribution (acid test).** The developer or a partner, in its own words, ties AI or machine learning to **this molecule**:
- the source names the molecule, or is unambiguously about it (for example, a release announcing that molecule's first dosing);
- the words are the developer's or partner's: its website, release, filing, presentation, or a paper or patent it authored (`developer_paper` counts here; it does not make the row attribution tier A, which needs independent documentation);
- the AI is named as AI, machine learning, deep learning, generative or neural models, or a named tool the same developer describes as AI or ML (for example "Chemistry42"). "Computational", "in silico", "physics-based", "structure-based", "free-energy perturbation", "molecular dynamics" and "rational design" are not AI.

Fails rule 1: the claim is only about the company or platform and never connects to the molecule; or only third parties make it.

**Rule 2: materiality.** The claim says **what the AI did** for this molecule, in one of two steps:
- **target:** AI or ML identified, nominated, predicted or prioritised the target, or the target–disease link, that this molecule acts on;
- **design:** AI or ML generated, designed, optimised or engineered the molecule's structure or sequence, or selected the clinical compound or its chemical starting point from a set it scored (for example ML virtual screening that produced the series).

Wording rules:
- **Passes:** "designed by/with/using [AI]", "AI-designed", "generated by [generative AI]", "optimised with [ML]", "[AI] identified the target", "AI-predicted target", "AI modeling revealed the target".
- **Fails** (platform language, no step): "discovered using/with our AI platform", "developed using", "derived from", "emerged from", "came from", "powered by", "enabled by", "leveraging", "from our AI-enabled platform", "AI-discovered" with no step, "program from our AI platform".
- ML used only for screening logistics, ADMET or property filtering among many gates, formulation, delivery vehicles, patient selection, biomarkers or trial operations fails.

Fails rule 2 → borderline `platform_only` (if rule 1 passes) or the code that fits better (§ 4).

**Rule 3: dosed in humans by 20 Oct 2026.** Any one of:
- a trial registry record (ClinicalTrials.gov, China CTR, ChiCTR, CTIS/EU CTR, ANZCTR, jRCT, CRIS, PACTR) with actual enrolment above zero, or an actual (not estimated) start date together with a recruiting, active or completed status;
- a dated developer, partner or regulator statement that a first participant was dosed, or that a trial started dosing or completed;
- a developer-authored paper reporting human dosing results.

Fails: IND or CTA clearance alone; "expected to start"; a registry record with an estimated start only; withdrawn with zero enrolment.

**Rule 4: red team.** A second reviewer argues for exclusion and searches for evidence that:
- the molecule predates the AI step claimed (a legacy compound or an in-licensed asset, with the AI claim retrofitted later);
- the developer's own detailed account (paper, patent, filing) describes the design or target choice without ML, contradicting the headline claim;
- the claim is about a sibling, back-up or the programme rather than this molecule;
- the AI step was minor (one filter among many), or AI was used only after the molecule was chosen;
- the claim was withdrawn, or the developer now describes the molecule without AI;
- dosing is not actually confirmed.

The row survives if the red team finds **no evidence** for these. An argument without evidence does not defeat a row. Evidence that defeats rule 1 or 2 moves the row to borderline with the matching code; evidence that defeats rule 3, or shows AI played no part, moves it to excluded.

## 4. Borderline: dosed, an AI connection, not strict

| Code | Use when |
|---|---|
| `physics_only` | The design the developer describes for this molecule is computational but not ML (physics-based, structure-based, FEP, molecular dynamics), even if the platform also uses ML. |
| `platform_only` | The developer or partner connects this molecule to an AI or ML platform, but never says what the AI did for it (fails rule 2), or the platform is described as AI only in general. |
| `third_party_only` | Only third parties make a molecule-level AI claim; the developer is silent. |
| `repurposing` | AI or ML picked a new indication or combination for an existing drug. Borderline even if the claim passes rules 1 and 2. |
| `vaccine_antigen` | AI or ML selected vaccine antigens or neoantigens (including personalised vaccines). Borderline even if the claim passes rules 1 and 2. |
| `other` | Anything else; the note says what (for example a scope question in § 6 decided as borderline). |

Where two codes fit, use the first that applies in this order: `repurposing`, `vaccine_antigen`, `physics_only`, `third_party_only`, `platform_only`, `other`.

The difference between `platform_only` (borderline) and `no_ai_link` (excluded): borderline needs a developer or partner statement that connects this molecule to the AI platform (the molecule named alongside the AI platform). AI language about the company only, never linked to the molecule, is excluded.

## 5. Excluded rows

Each exclusion reason is re-tested against facts current on 20 Oct 2026, molecule by molecule, and given one code:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `not_dosed` | No dosing evidence by the as-of date (rule 3 fails). |
| `no_ai_link` | No developer, partner or third-party claim connects AI to this molecule; or AI language is company-level only. |
| `ai_disclaimed` | The developer says AI played no part, or its own account shows the molecule came from elsewhere. |
| `in_licensed` | Discovered elsewhere; AI used only after acquisition (development, patient selection). |
| `legacy_retrofit` | A legacy molecule whose AI claim was added later, with no contemporaneous ML evidence. |
| `out_of_scope` | Not a drug candidate the registry covers: formulation of an existing active, delivery vehicle, observational study, nutraceutical, AI used for analytics or dosing, or a scope question in § 6 decided as out. |

If a re-test finds the reason no longer holds (for example a `not_dosed` molecule is now dosed), the molecule is coded under § 3 as if new.

## 7. Process

1. **Coders.** Two independent coders per row, blind to each other and to the current tier, notes and `ai_role`. They see the molecule name, developer, partner, modality, target, indication and the current source URLs as leads only. Each records the § 2 fields, a pass/fail for rules 1 to 3 with the evidence, the tier and code, and a confidence (high, medium, low).
2. **Red team** (rule 4) on every row either coder passes as strict.
3. **Adjudication.** Rows where both coders and the red team agree take that tier. Every disagreement on tier, and every proposed move into or out of strict, goes to John with the evidence (`docs/REVIEW-SIGNOFF.md`).
4. **Verification.** Before anything is written to the CSVs, the maintainer's session re-fetches every `ai_claim_url` and checks that the quote is on the page. A quote not found is not used, and the row is re-coded without it.
5. **Agreement** is reported as percent agreement and Cohen's kappa on the tier (strict, borderline, excluded) and on the borderline code.

## 8. What this does not change

Target precedence, status, phase and attribution tier rules (METHODOLOGY § 3) are unchanged. Attribution tier A still needs documentation independent of the developer.
